Customs Excellence & Transformation Case Study by Anna Kudyba

Customs Control Tower

What happens when business growth makes daily operations unnecessarily more complex? And, in the long game, financially risky?

The goal is to reduce inconsistencies at the source, detect remaining mismatches early, prioritize exceptions by risk, and create audit-ready evidence for better customs control.

Stakeholders Handshake

Usually teams disagree because they optimize for different risks: finance wants cost clarity, operations wants speed, customs wants compliance, tech wants clean requirements, and leadership wants progress.

The united dashboards will answer the question: what decision are we making, what data do we need and trust, what risk are we accepting, and who owns the next step?

Because the unit of outcome is decision, not data.

01 / Document flow Scroll to explore

Customs team needs all shipment documents in one controlled flow.

Each document shows a different business view. The control point is whether they align before clearance, not after a broker query, reconciliation issue or audit.

5 linked checkpoints
01 / Document flow

Start with the shipment story.

PO, CI, IC and CD should be connected at shipment and line-item level. This creates one view of commercial intent, financial flow, declared customs position and clearance outcome. Mismatches between these documents become early warning signals.

  • PO defines the commercial intent.
  • CI confirms the external transaction.
  • IC reflects the internal financial flow.
  • CD becomes the official customs record.
  • Clearance shows the operational outcome.
02 / Control logic

Monitoring should track three layers.

The dashboard should separate what is missing, what is risky, and what creates delay, cost, or audit issues, helping the team stay technically accurate and organizationally effective.

  • Data Quality: completeness, availability, duplicates, late documents
  • Compliance Risk: HS code, origin, value, quantity, declaration accuracy
  • Impact: delay, manual review, duty variance, broker response, audit readiness
03 / Risk score

Prioritize by impact, not by volume.

Each exception is ranked by the risk it creates for the business:

  • compliance exposure
  • duty or cost impact
  • clearance urgency
  • repeat issue pattern
  • audit relevance
04 / Dashboard views

Five views, clearer decisions.

The dashboard should be structured as role-based views, each answering a different operational question from the same trusted data foundation.

One data foundation should support different decision moments.

  • Leadership needs control and trend visibility.
  • Customs needs a daily action queue.
  • Compliance needs risk patterns and evidence.
  • Finance needs to see where mismatches create cost impact.
  • Operations needs delay signals and root causes.
05 / Future capabilities

Automation works best when the control logic is already trusted.

The roadmap starts with reliable shipment-level visibility, then adds exception management, audit evidence, and targeted AI support. Predictive alerts come later, once the matching rules, ownership model, and historical data are strong enough to trust.

  • Visibility: Are the documents complete and aligned?
  • Control: Who owns the exception and evidence?
  • Automation: What can be validated or routed automatically?
  • Prediction: Where are delay or duty risks likely to appear?

Dashboard proposal

Five views, one operating model.

A

Executive Control Tower

Control health, shipment volume, high-risk exceptions, duty exposure, clearance performance and audit readiness.

Are we in control?
B

Data Quality & Matching

Document availability, field completeness, duplicate or late documents, and matching logic across PO, CI, IC and CD.

Can we trust the data?
C

Exception Management

Prioritized review queue with mismatch reason, risk score, owner, status, due date, aging and shipment-level drilldown.

What needs action now?
D

Compliance Risk

HS and origin risk, high-risk lanes, repeat issue patterns, control gaps and audit evidence completeness.

Where is risk increasing?
E

Financial Exposure

Duty paid vs expected duty, customs value variance, correction candidates, refund potential and exposure by lane.

Where is cost exposure?

Summary

The proposal positions customs data as the foundation for a strong, scalable operating model.

By connecting PO, CI, IC and CD data, the Customs team can create one trusted view of shipment quality, compliance risk, financial exposure and operational issues. Built on the existing BigQuery and Looker foundation, the dashboard helps the team decide what to review, what to escalate, what to automate, and where recurring issues need to be fixed.

Start with trusted control logic. Build the dashboard around action. Automate where the process is ready.
